Use a Zipper Merge

When two congested lanes become one, an orderly late merge can shorten the backup and remove the argument over who is “cutting.”

Recognize the right situation

A zipper merge is commonly used where a lane closes and traffic is heavy and slow. Signs may say Use Both Lanes, Stay in Lane to Merge Point, or Merge Here—Take Turns.

Do not assume every lane reduction calls for a late merge. Follow the posted signs, flaggers, and current traffic conditions.

How the zipper works

1

Use both open lanes

Stay in your lane at a safe, steady speed until the marked merge point. Do not race ahead or straddle lanes to block others.

2

Prepare before the taper

Reduce speed, increase following distance, check mirrors, and signal. Leave enough room for one vehicle to enter.

3

Alternate one at a time

At the merge point, one vehicle proceeds from the open lane, then one from the ending lane—like interlocking zipper teeth.

4

Protect safety, not position

Do not force a gap, accelerate to block someone, use the shoulder, or “teach” another driver a lesson. Adjust and merge when safe.

The rule that prevents confusion

Use both lanes when directed, merge at the marked point, take turns, and remain predictable.

When traffic is moving freely or signs direct an earlier merge, merge earlier when a safe gap is available. The signs choose the method; your ego does not.
