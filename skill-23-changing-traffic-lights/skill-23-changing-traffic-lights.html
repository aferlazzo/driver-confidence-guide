Handle Changing Traffic Lights

A yellow light is a warning, not a starting pistol. A fresh green is permission to proceed—but only after confirming the intersection is clear.

Approach every signal with a plan

As you approach, keep a safe following distance and notice whether the green has been visible for a while. A “stale green” may change soon, but it is only a clue—not a timer.

Check traffic ahead and behind, ease off the accelerator when conditions suggest a change, and decide where you could stop smoothly if yellow appears.

What each moment requires

🟡

Yellow with room to stop

Brake smoothly and stop before the crosswalk or limit line. Do not accelerate simply to beat the red.

🟡

Yellow without room to stop safely

Do not panic-stop or race. Continue under control and clear the intersection promptly.

🔴

Red means stop

Stop before the marked line, crosswalk, or intersection. Do not creep into the pedestrian space while waiting.

🟢

Green means go—if clear

Scan left, right, and ahead before moving. Check for late cross traffic, turning vehicles, pedestrians, cyclists, and a blocked path.

Do not enter a box you cannot leave

Even with a green signal, remain behind the line if traffic is backed up and there is no room beyond the intersection. Blocking the intersection turns one traffic jam into four.

The safe question is not “Can I beat the light?” It is “Can I stop safely—or clear the intersection safely?”
