Tucson driving

Understand HAWK Signals

The signal that goes dark—and the flashing red that still requires a stop.

What is a HAWK?

A HAWK, officially called a Pedestrian Hybrid Beacon, helps people cross busy roads. It has two red lenses above one yellow lens and normally remains dark until someone activates it.

The first HAWK was developed in Tucson in 2000. Visitors may never have encountered one, which makes the sequence worth learning before it suddenly appears over six lanes of traffic.

1 · DARK

Proceed cautiously

The beacon is inactive. Continue carefully, watch the crosswalk, and yield to pedestrians as required.

2 · FLASHING YELLOW

Slow down

A pedestrian activated the beacon. Reduce speed and prepare for the signal to change.

3 · STEADY YELLOW

Prepare to stop

Stop if you can do so safely. Do not accelerate to beat the red.

4 · STEADY RED

Stop and remain stopped

Come to a complete stop at the stop line. Pedestrians have the protected crossing interval.

5 · ALTERNATING FLASHING RED

Stop, then proceed only when clear

Every driver must make a complete stop. After stopping, proceed only after pedestrians have cleared your side of the roadway. If someone is still crossing your lane, stay stopped.

The memory shortcut

Dark: careful. Flashing yellow: slow. Yellow: prepare to stop. Solid red: stay. Flashing red: stop, check, then go only when clear.

Do not simply follow the car ahead through a flashing red. Each vehicle must stop independently.

Official guidance

This guide follows the Arizona Department of Transportation PHB guidance and the Federal Highway Administration HAWK sequence. Always obey posted signs, traffic-control devices, law enforcement, and current Arizona law.
