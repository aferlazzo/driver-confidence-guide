Adventure 18 · Entering and Exiting I-10 · Alex

Alex Joins I-10

Alex needs to travel across Tucson on I-10. The freeway has generous signs, long ramps, and drivers who occasionally treat both as optional reading.

Scene 1 of 5
Completed decisions: 0/5

Scene 1 · Before the ramp

The freeway begins before the freeway

Alex confirms his route, sees the entrance sign, and notices fast traffic already on I-10.

His phone is mounted and speaking directions. He is still tempted to zoom the map with two fingers while moving.

What should Alex do?

Alex arrives prepared

He knows where he is going and can devote his attention to speed, space, and traffic.

The phone knows the route, not the traffic

Alex leaves it mounted and looks through the windshield. Directions can repeat themselves; collisions are less accommodating.

I-10 is a poor brainstorming room

Alex confirms the route before adding freeway decisions.

Scene 2 · The acceleration lane

Alex must join moving traffic

The ramp opens into a long acceleration lane. Freeway traffic is moving near the posted limit with a usable gap behind a white SUV.

Alex is traveling much slower and wonders whether stopping at the end of the ramp would simplify matters.

What is the better plan?

The ramp does its job

Alex reaches a compatible speed and merges into a planned gap without forcing another driver to brake hard.

The ramp is not a waiting room

Unless traffic or a signal requires stopping, Alex uses the acceleration lane to match traffic and find a gap.

A turn signal is not a royal decree

Alex must select a usable gap and adjust his own speed. Merging traffic does not gain automatic right-of-way.

Scene 3 · One lane over

A truck blocks Alex's forward view

Alex wants to move left. His mirror appears clear, but a compact car is traveling beside his rear quarter panel—inside the blind spot.

How should he change lanes?

The shoulder check finds the missing car

Alex lets it pass, checks again, and makes one smooth lane change.

Invisible is not absent

Mirrors leave blind spots. Alex checks over his shoulder before moving.

The signal communicates; it does not reserve

Alex waits until the lane is clear and then moves gradually.

Scene 4 · Cruising room

Traffic enters from the next ramp

Alex is in the right lane. Two cars are accelerating alongside him on the entrance ramp. The lane to his left is open.

What should Alex do?

Cooperation keeps traffic flowing

Alex creates space without making a sudden move. Courtesy works best when paired with awareness.

The freeway is not a property dispute

Alex creates a safe gap rather than trapping the entering driver.

Sudden generosity is still sudden

Alex checks around him and changes lane or speed smoothly.

Scene 5 · Exit ahead

Alex is two lanes left of his exit

The exit is one mile away. Traffic is busy but gaps exist. Alex briefly considers remaining where he is until the last 200 feet.

What is the safe exit plan?

Early planning produces a boring exit

Alex reaches the exit lane in stages and decelerates on the ramp. Boring is an excellent freeway result.

An exit is not worth a collision

Alex moves over early. If he cannot exit safely, he continues to the next exit instead of improvising pavement.

The travel lane is still traveling

Alex reaches the exit lane first, then reduces speed on the ramp.

Adventure complete

Alex enters, travels, and exits without alarming I-10
