Adventure 17 · Railroad Crossings · Tyler

Tyler versus the Tracks

Cortaro Road at I-10 combines ramps, signals, impatient traffic, and an active railroad crossing. Tyler brings snacks and absolutely no awareness of where the car is.

Scene 1 of 5
Completed decisions: 0/5

Scene 1 · Too many moving parts

Traffic stacks up beside the railroad

Tyler approaches the Cortaro crossing near I-10. Cars are merging from the freeway, a traffic signal is ahead, and brake lights stretch across the tracks.

His passenger asks whether he sees the round railroad warning sign. Tyler says, “I saw a burrito place.”

What should Tyler do first?

Tyler expands the picture

He notices the rails, warning equipment, backed-up traffic, and the space beyond the crossing. A safe decision starts before the tracks.

The bumper ahead has no teaching certificate

The other driver may misjudge the available room. Tyler must read the whole crossing for himself.

The burrito can wait; freight cannot

Tyler puts the destination aside and scans the crossing, signals, and traffic beyond the rails.

Scene 2 · A green light, but no landing space

Traffic moves one car length

The road signal turns green, but vehicles beyond the tracks stop again. There is not enough room for Tyler's whole car to clear the nearest rail.

“Go,” Tyler says to himself. “The light is green, and trains probably have a schedule.”

What is the safe choice?

Green does not guarantee an exit

Tyler waits. Freight trains do not run on a schedule drivers can rely upon, and a vehicle must never be trapped on the tracks.

A green light cannot lengthen the road

If Tyler cannot clear every rail, he does not enter. Right-of-way is not a safe parking spot.

The train neglected to consult Tyler

Drivers should expect a train on any track at any time. Tyler waits until the far side has enough room.

Scene 3 · The opening

Enough space finally appears

Several cars move through the next signal. Tyler can now cross every rail and leave a safe gap between his rear bumper and the nearest rail.

The crossing lights are off and the gates are fully raised.

How should he cross?

Look, commit, clear

Tyler crosses in one continuous movement and leaves the tracks completely behind him.

The tracks are not a decision lounge

Tyler decides before entering. Once safe to cross, he proceeds without stopping.

The map cannot see the locomotive

Tyler keeps his eyes on the crossing and checks navigation only after he is safely clear.

Scene 4 · Lights, bells, gates

The crossing activates on the return trip

Tyler is safely behind the stop line when red lights flash, bells sound, and the gates begin to lower.

The lane beside him is empty. Tyler wonders whether going around the gate would save at least forty-five seconds.

What should Tyler do?

The wait is shorter than the consequences

Tyler stays put, watches for another train, and moves only after every warning ends and the crossing is clear.

The gate is not decorative

Driving around or under a lowered gate is illegal and deadly. Tyler remains stopped.

One train can hide another

Tyler waits until the lights stop, the gate rises fully, and every track is clear.

Scene 5 · The emergency Tyler must know

What if a vehicle stalls on the tracks?

Safely parked later, the crew reviews the worst case. Tyler suggests trying the ignition repeatedly because leaving a car feels “a little dramatic.”

No train is visible—but that does not make the tracks safe.

What should everyone do first?

People first. Always.

A stalled vehicle is replaceable. Tyler gets everyone out and away even when no train is visible, then reports the crossing ID from a safe location.

The car does not get a vote

Everyone exits immediately and moves far from the tracks. Property comes after people.

Do not turn one trapped object into several

Everyone gets away from the tracks first, then contacts the railroad using the emergency sign or calls 911.
