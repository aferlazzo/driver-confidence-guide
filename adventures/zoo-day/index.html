Adventure 2 · Mental Rehearsal

Go to the Zoo

You are driving Maya’s car across town with Alex, Tyler, and Emma. The animals are mostly contained. Your passengers’ opinions are not.

Practice your decisions here, before they matter out there.

Scene 1 of 5
Confidence points: 0/5

Scene 1 · Before departure

Your phone has 14% battery

Emma has the tickets.

Alex has snacks.

Tyler has binoculars powerful enough to inspect the moon.

Your phone has fourteen percent battery.

The zoo is across town, construction has closed the usual exit, and nobody brought a charging cable.

“Fourteen percent is practically fifteen.” — Tyler

What would you do before leaving?

You leave with a route and a backup

Emma downloads the route on her phone.

You review the detour before moving.

Your phone stays available for calls.

Tyler announces that he also knew the way, then points north while facing west.

The backup plan depends on finding a store

The battery drops to eleven percent before you reach the construction zone.

Now you need directions and a charging cable at the same time.

You pull into a parking lot and move the route to Emma’s charged phone.

Two people become distracted

Emma studies the phone.

You glance toward her when she hesitates.

You miss the temporary detour sign.

You pull over and settle the route while stopped.

The signs begin after the confusing part

The normal exit is blocked.

The detour sends you toward an unfamiliar frontage road.

You pull into a safe lot and set the route before guessing again.

Scene 2 · The fuel gauge

The range estimate drops to 19 miles

Traffic slows.

The low-fuel light appears.

The estimated range falls from 28 miles to 19.

The zoo is 11 miles away, but the return trip has not disappeared.

“We can get gas later. The car will be lighter after we eat the snacks.” — Tyler

What would you do?

Five minutes removes the problem

You choose a station directly along the detour.

You refuel before the zoo crowds and parking decisions compete for attention.

Alex buys gum.

Emma points out that gum is not lunch.

Open Skill: Pump Gas Correctly Opens in a new tab; your Adventure stays here.

The fuel problem follows you inside

You reach the zoo with twelve miles showing.

Instead of enjoying the visit, you keep wondering whether nearby stations will still be open and crowded later.

You decide to refuel before parking.

The estimate changes faster than expected

Stop-and-go traffic pushes the number down to thirteen miles.

The distance to the zoo has barely changed.

You exit for fuel before the warning becomes an emergency.

The shortcut adds uncertainty

The road narrows and the speed drops.

You are using more time while depending on a shrinking estimate.

You return to the known route and stop for fuel.

Scene 3 · Parking

Lot G looks like every other lot

You find a space near a cheerful cartoon giraffe.

Everyone climbs out while debating which animal would make the worst roommate.

The entrance is several rows away.

“We will remember the giraffe. Giraffes are extremely memorable.” — Alex

What would you do before walking away?

Thirty seconds prevents a long search

You photograph “Giraffe G-14.”

You save the location.

You notice one rear window is still open and close it before locking the car.

Tyler asks whether photographing a giraffe steals part of its soul.

There is more than one giraffe

Before reaching the entrance, you pass two more giraffe markers.

The first one is already hard to distinguish.

You walk back and record G-14.

The pin remembers the area, not the open window

You take three steps and glance back.

The rear window is still open.

You return, secure the car, and photograph the row marker too.

The entrance route bends

Construction fencing redirects pedestrians through another aisle.

Your row count is already unreliable.

You return and photograph the marker.

Scene 4 · The bird incident

Your windshield takes a direct hit

Several hours later, everyone returns tired and sunburned.

A bird has left an impressive contribution directly in your line of sight.

You use the windshield washer.

The edges clear, but the center becomes a wide smear.

“Technically, we wanted an authentic wildlife experience.” — Emma

What would you do?

You restore the full view

Alex gets water.

You clean the glass until the entire driving area is clear.

Tyler offers the binoculars as a replacement windshield and is overruled.

Open Skill: Clean the Windshield Opens in a new tab; your Adventure stays here.

The clear corner is not enough

You sit behind the wheel.

The smear hides part of the crosswalk and the front-left corner of the lot.

You shut the engine off and clean the glass.

More fluid spreads the problem

The smear becomes thinner but wider.

Sunlight catches it and creates glare.

You stop wasting washer fluid and clean the windshield by hand.

A passenger cannot replace your view

Alex can describe one side of the lot.

He cannot see exactly what you need from the driver’s seat.

You clean the obstruction before moving.

Scene 5 · The hot car

The temperature gauge begins climbing

Traffic stalls on the return trip.

The temperature gauge rises above its normal position.

A hot smell enters through the vents.

The shoulder begins several hundred feet ahead.

“Maybe the car is remembering the reptile house.” — Tyler

What would you do?

You stop before heat becomes damage

You turn off the air conditioner.

You reach a safe area and shut down the engine before the gauge enters the red zone.

Roadside assistance finds a coolant leak.

The group waits in the shade and ranks zoo animals by likelihood of owning a timeshare.

Open Skill: Know When to Call for Help Opens in a new tab; your Adventure stays here.

Traffic does not become cooling air

The lane moves for a few seconds, then stops again.

The gauge continues rising.

You turn off the air conditioner and reach a safe stopping place.

The shoulder is unsafe and the engine is hot

Traffic passes within feet of the car.

The radiator cap may release scalding coolant.

You continue only far enough to reach a safer area, then shut down and call for help.

Watching the gauge does not cool the engine

The needle keeps climbing.

The hot smell grows stronger.

You stop safely before the engine forces the decision for you.

Mental rehearsal complete

You handled the trip before it happened

You earned 0 of 5 confidence points.

  • Set navigation and a backup before moving.
  • Refuel before low fuel controls the trip.
  • Record where you park and secure the car.
  • Do not drive with obstructed visibility.
  • Stop safely when an engine may be overheating.
