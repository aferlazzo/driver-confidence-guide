Adventure 19 · Passing and Speed · Alex

Alex and the Fast-Lane Myth

Back on I-10, Alex encounters a slow truck, a speeding pack, and a pickup that communicates entirely through grille size.

Scene 1 of 5
Completed decisions: 0/5

Scene 1 · Choose a home lane

I-10 opens into three lanes

Alex is not passing and his exit is several miles away. The center and right lanes are moving smoothly.

Where should he cruise?

Alex chooses flow over status

He uses the lane that fits his trip. The far-left lane is a passing tool, not premium seating.

The left lane is not an apartment

Camping there obstructs drivers who are overtaking and encourages unsafe passing on the right.

Lane roulette adds risk, not progress

Alex chooses a stable lane and changes only for a clear reason.

Scene 2 · A legitimate pass

A truck is moving below the surrounding flow

The left lane is clear. Alex has enough room to pass without exceeding the posted speed limit.

How should he pass?

Pass completed; lane released

Alex overtakes deliberately and returns right without cutting off the truck.

A hypothetical truck does not need a reserved lane

Once the pass is complete and a safe gap exists, Alex returns right.

The speed limit survived the lane change

Passing does not authorize speeding. Alex waits for a pass he can complete lawfully.

Scene 3 · The grille in the mirror

A pickup tailgates while Alex is passing

Alex is alongside the truck and cannot safely move right yet. The pickup behind him closes the gap and flashes its lights.

What should Alex do?

Alex refuses the argument

He does not race or retaliate. He completes the maneuver safely and lets the pickup continue elsewhere.

The tailgater does not set Alex's speed

Alex maintains lawful control and moves right when safe.

Brake-checking teaches only collision physics

Alex stays predictable and creates distance from the aggressive driver.

Scene 4 · Everyone else is speeding

A pack rushes past Alex

Several drivers are well above the posted limit. Alex feels slow even though he is traveling at a reasonable speed with the right lane available.

Should he match them?

The crowd does not rewrite the sign

Alex controls his own speed and position. He does not obstruct or compete.

Speeding is not safer in a committee

Alex follows the posted limit and adjusts downward when traffic, visibility, or weather requires it.

Alex is not the volunteer pace car

He keeps right when not passing instead of deliberately blocking the left lane.

Scene 5 · A missed opening

Alex wants to pass again, but the gap is tight

A car occupies his blind spot and another approaches quickly in the left lane. Waiting will cost perhaps twenty seconds.

What should Alex do?

The safe gap arrives

Alex waits, checks every zone, and changes lanes without making anyone else solve his problem.

A signal cannot enlarge a gap

Alex waits. Missing one opportunity is cheaper than occupying the same lane as another car.

Ambiguity is not a maneuver

Alex maintains his lane until a complete, deliberate change is safe.

Adventure complete

Alex retires from the imaginary I-10 racing league
