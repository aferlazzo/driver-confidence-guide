The Tucson Signal That Goes Dark

Tyler’s cousin Jordan is visiting from Ohio. Jordan expects sunshine, cactus photographs, and ordinary traffic lights. Tucson has arranged a three-lens surprise.

Scene 1 of 5
Completed decisions: 0/5

Scene 1 · The dark signal

Jordan sees a traffic signal that appears to be turned off

The unusual signal hangs above a marked pedestrian crossing: two red lenses over one yellow lens. All three are dark.

“Either Tucson has invented minimalist traffic lights, or we missed a memo.” — Jordan

What should Jordan do?

Dark does not mean broken

Jordan continues carefully. The beacon remains dark because nobody has requested the crossing.

Jordan begins a new life at the stop line

Scene 2 · Flashing yellow

The bottom yellow light begins flashing

A pedestrian has pressed the crossing button. Jordan is still well back from the crosswalk.

“The minimalist phase has ended.” — Tyler

What does flashing yellow require?

The warning gets time to work

Jordan eases off the accelerator and has plenty of distance when the signal changes.

The signal is not his hype man

The yellow becomes steady almost immediately. Jordan brakes harder than necessary and adopts the radical strategy of slowing down.

Scene 3 · Steady yellow

The flashing stops and yellow remains solid

Jordan is approaching at a normal speed with enough room to stop safely. The two red lenses wait above like extremely obvious foreshadowing.

What should he do?

Predictable beats exciting

Jordan stops smoothly. A moment later, both red lights illuminate and the pedestrian receives the WALK signal.

Red has resolved its doubts

The red lights appear before Jordan reaches the line. He stops, now with more drama and less dignity.

Scene 4 · Solid red

Both upper red lights are steady

A woman and child enter the crosswalk. The opposite half of the road looks empty, but they are still crossing Jordan’s lanes.

What does solid red mean here?

Solid red means exactly what it looks like

Jordan stays stopped. The pedestrians continue without having to negotiate with a slowly advancing bumper.

The bumper enters the conversation

The pedestrians hesitate. Jordan stops fully and learns that “creeping” is still movement toward a person in a crosswalk.

Scene 5 · Alternating flashing red

The two red lights begin flashing back and forth

The pedestrians have cleared Jordan’s side of the roadway and are finishing the far side. The car ahead stops, checks, and proceeds. Jordan follows next.

“This is the part visitors get wrong.” — Tyler

May Jordan simply follow the car ahead?

Every car stops; clear lanes may proceed

Jordan stops independently, confirms his lanes are clear, and proceeds. The signal soon goes dark again.

Traffic law declines the group rate

Tyler stops him. During alternating flashing red, each successive driver must stop and check independently.
