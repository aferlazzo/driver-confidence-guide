Adventure 1 · Enhanced Edition

Borrow Mom's Car

Alex needs wheels for an afternoon with Tyler. Mom supplies the keys, three instructions, and the unnerving confidence of someone lending out a machine she still owns.

Scene 1 of 5
Confidence points: 0/5

Scene 1 · The handoff

Keys, conditions, and one unfamiliar dashboard

Mom places the keys on the counter as though beginning a hostage negotiation.

“Bring it back with gas. No drive-through car wash. And the radio stays below ‘earthquake.’” — Mom

Outside, Tyler is already taking selfies beside the car.

“It has four wheels and a steering wheel. We have mastered the concept.” — Tyler

Alex sits down and discovers a push-button start, an electronic parking brake, unfamiliar headlights, and enough steering-wheel buttons to launch a modest satellite.

What should Alex do before moving?

Good call

Alex finds the lights, wipers, parking brake, fuel-door release, and owner’s manual. Mom points out that the shifter returns to center after every selection—something Tyler’s theory did not cover.

The car votes no

Alex backs six feet with a mirror aimed at the headliner and the parking brake still engaged. The dashboard chimes like a game show where every answer is wrong. They stop and do the two-minute check after all.

Scene 2 · The phone problem

Tyler wants music. Alex needs directions.

They reach the end of the block. Tyler announces that the car’s audio system is “emotionally unavailable” because it has not paired with his phone.

“Keep driving. I’ll press things until technology respects me.” — Tyler

The map is not connected, the next turn is approaching, and the touchscreen has six menus named with words no normal person uses in conversation.

What should Alex do?

Park first, poke second

In a grocery-store lot, Alex connects the phone, confirms the route, and starts a playlist. Tyler objects to the playlist but survives this grave injustice.

Too many copilots

Tyler changes the language to French and Alex misses the turn. They pull over, restore English, and add seven minutes to the trip. The car now knows the word for “settings” in two languages.

Scene 3 · The fuel stop

Which side? Which fuel? Whose dignity?

The fuel gauge is low, exactly as Mom predicted. Alex approaches a crowded station while Tyler provides commentary without useful information.

“The hose probably stretches to either side. Probably is a powerful word.” — Tyler

Alex remembers the tiny arrow beside the fuel-pump icon but does not remember which gasoline grade Mom’s car uses.

What is the smart sequence?

No guessing required

The arrow puts the fuel door beside the pump. The label says regular unleaded. Alex fills the tank without dragging a hose across the trunk like a giant rubber scarf.

Pressure is not a fuel grade

Alex stops before pressing the button and reads the label. The waiting driver is not judging anyone; he is eating a breakfast burrito with astonishing commitment.

Scene 4 · The warning light

A yellow horseshoe appears

Ten minutes later, a yellow tire-pressure symbol lights up. The car feels normal. Tyler leans toward the dashboard.

“Maybe it means the car found a tiny horseshoe. Lucky us.” — Tyler

Alex knows a warning light is information, not decoration, but they are only fifteen minutes from their destination.

What should Alex do?

Investigate before escalating

No tire looks flat. The manual confirms the symbol, and the gas station has an air pump. One tire is several PSI low. Alex inflates it to the door-jamb specification and makes a note to tell Mom.

The light does not get bored

After two miles, Alex’s imagination supplies seventeen possible tire failures. They pull over anyway, find the low tire, and handle it calmly. Worry was optional; checking was useful.

Scene 5 · The return

Bring it back better than you found it

They return on time, with a full tank and a functioning playlist. There is one final question: what should Alex tell Mom?

“We could just hand over the keys and let the dashboard tell the story.” — Tyler

How should Alex finish the handoff?

Trust returned with the keys

Mom appreciates the details and schedules a tire inspection. She also notices the tank is full. Alex has not merely borrowed a car; Alex has demonstrated that borrowing includes responsibility.

The dashboard is a snitch

Mom sees the warning history and asks what happened. Alex explains eventually, but the delayed truth makes a small problem feel larger. Clear handoffs prevent that.

Adventure complete

The car came home. So did everyone else.

You earned 0 of 5 confidence points.

  • Learn unfamiliar controls before the car moves.
  • Park before using menus or connecting a phone.
  • Confirm fuel type instead of guessing.
  • Treat warning lights as useful information.
  • Return borrowed property with a clear handoff.
