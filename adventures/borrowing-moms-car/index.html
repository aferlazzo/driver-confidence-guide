Adventure 1 · Mental Rehearsal

Borrow Mom's Car

You have the keys. Tyler is coming along. Your job is simple: return the car, the passengers, and Mom's trust in good condition.

Practice your decisions here, before they matter out there.

Scene 1 of 5
Confidence points: 0/5

Scene 1 · The handoff

You have the keys

Mom hands you the keys.

“Bring it back with gas. No drive-through car wash. And keep the radio below earthquake.” — Mom

Tyler is already waiting beside the car.

“It has four wheels and a steering wheel. We are fully qualified.” — Tyler

You sit down.

The start button is unfamiliar.

The parking brake is electronic.

The mirrors and seat are set for someone else.

What would you do before moving?

You remove the guesswork

You adjust the seat and mirrors.

You locate the lights, wipers, parking brake, fuel-door release, and owner's manual.

Mom shows you that the shifter returns to the center after each selection.

Tyler's four-wheel qualification did not cover that.

One problem remains hidden

You can see clearly and reach the pedals.

At the end of the driveway, you reach for the parking brake and hesitate.

The switch does not work the way you expected.

You stop and learn the unfamiliar controls before entering traffic.

The phone can wait

Your route is ready.

Your mirrors are not.

When you look behind you, the rearview mirror shows the ceiling.

You put the phone down and complete the pre-drive check.

The car objects

You start backing up.

The rearview mirror is still pointed at the ceiling.

The parking brake is still on.

The dashboard begins chiming.

Tyler looks over. “I think the car wants a second opinion.”

You stop and do the two-minute check.

Scene 2 · The phone problem

The next turn is approaching

You reach the end of the block.

The phone is not connected.

The map is not on the dashboard.

Tyler begins opening menus on the touchscreen.

“Keep driving. I will press things until technology respects me.” — Tyler

Your next turn is less than a minute away.

What would you do next?

You stop dividing your attention

You pull into a grocery-store parking lot.

You put the car in Park.

You connect the phone and confirm the route.

Tyler objects to the playlist but survives.

Two people become distracted

Tyler changes the screen language.

You glance over to help.

You miss the turn.

You pull into a parking lot and finish the setup while stopped.

The directions compete with the road

Tyler looks down at the phone.

He calls out the turn late.

You brake harder than necessary and continue past it.

You park before trying again.

General direction is not a route

You continue for several blocks.

The street names stop looking familiar.

You pull over before the guess becomes a longer detour.

Scene 3 · The fuel stop

The gauge is low

Mom was right about the fuel.

The station is crowded.

You see a small arrow beside the fuel-pump symbol.

You do not remember which fuel grade the car uses.

“The hose probably reaches either side.” — Tyler

What would you do at the station?

You verify instead of guessing

The arrow puts the fuel door beside the pump.

The label says regular unleaded.

You fill the tank without stretching the hose across the car.

Your guess may be right

Your hand stops above the regular button.

You realize “usually” is not the same as “this car.”

You check the label before continuing.

Middle grade is not a safety setting

You position the car correctly.

The pump offers three grades.

Nothing on the pump tells you what this car requires.

You check the fuel-door label.

Calling works, but the answer is already nearby

Mom does not answer.

A driver is waiting behind you.

You notice the fuel label inside the open door and use it.

Scene 4 · The warning light

A yellow symbol appears

Ten minutes later, a yellow tire-pressure symbol lights up.

The car feels normal.

You are fifteen minutes from home.

“Maybe the car found a tiny horseshoe.” — Tyler

What would you do?

You investigate before deciding

You pull over in a safe place.

No tire looks flat.

The owner's manual confirms the symbol.

At the gas-station air pump, one tire measures several PSI low.

You inflate it to the door-jamb specification and plan to tell Mom.

Open Skill: Check Tire PressureOpens in a new tab; your Adventure stays here.

The warning stays with you

You continue driving.

Every small vibration now feels important.

After two miles, you pull over and inspect the tires.

You decide the check was worth doing before the worry grew.

Appearance does not reveal pressure

All four tires look nearly the same.

You would be guessing which one needs air and how much.

You check each tire with a gauge and use the door-jamb specification.

Resetting does not fix the cause

The menu offers a tire-pressure reset.

You still do not know whether a tire is low.

You inspect and measure the tires before touching the reset.

Scene 5 · The return

You hand back the keys

You return on time.

The tank is full.

The tire-pressure warning is no longer glowing.

Mom reaches for the keys.

“We could let the dashboard explain everything.” — Tyler

What would you tell Mom?

You return the information with the keys

You explain what happened.

Mom appreciates the details.

She schedules a tire inspection.

The full tank matters.

The honest handoff matters more.

Open Skill: Build a Check RoutineOpens in a new tab; your Adventure stays here.

The important detail is missing

Mom asks which tire was low and how low it was.

You realize “fine now” does not tell her whether the problem may return.

You give her the full explanation.

The receipt tells only half the story

Mom sees the full tank.

Later, she notices the warning history on the dashboard.

The delayed explanation makes a small problem feel larger.

Solving it once may not solve it

The tire loses pressure again the next morning.

Mom did not know to watch it.

You explain what happened and why she may need an inspection.

Mental rehearsal complete

You brought back more than the car

You earned 0 of 5 confidence points.

You practiced how to slow down, gather information, and make a better decision in an unfamiliar car.

  • Get familiar with an unfamiliar car before it moves.
  • Park before working with screens or phone connections.
  • Verify fuel requirements instead of relying on a guess.
  • Investigate warning lights before deciding what to do next.
  • Return borrowed property with a clear and honest handoff.
